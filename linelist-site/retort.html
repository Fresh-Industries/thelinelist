Retort Co-Packers for Pouches, Cans, and Trays

Last verified 21 Aug 2026 · Search volume: unpublished · Named plants only · MOQ only when the plant printed one

Use retort when the SKU is low-acid ambient — meal, broth, plant milk, pouch, can, or tray — pH greater than 4.6, commercially sterile. The kill step is a scheduled cook of the already-closed pack. Texture is cooked. That is the trade.

Do not send high-acid juice or hot sauce that can take a hot fill. Do not send refrigerated or never-heated SKUs (that is HPP). Spores survive HPP; HPP will not make low-acid soup ambient.

What retort actually is

Retort is a scheduled thermal process after seal. Product is filled into a hermetically sealed container, then the closed pack is cooked in a pressure vessel (the retort). The kill step is the cook of the already-closed pack, not the fill temperature. When the schedule is met, the pack is commercially sterile and ambient-stable: vegetative pathogens and bacterial spores (the reason low-acid canned food exists) are taken out of play.

Three plants publish that sequence on their own sites:

  • Berner: close the product, then cook it in a large vessel at high temperature under pressurization; after, it is microbiologically stable. Beverage steps: convert/cook → fill and close jars → retort sterilization → pack.
  • Thermal Kitchen: fill spouted pouches, seal, then cook in a pressurized water-spray vessel. They call the outcome commercially sterile, with up to an 18-month shelf life (vendor claim; your formula still needs its own study).
  • Big Brands: retort “sterilizes and pasteurizes” beverages they describe as pH greater than 4.5, “mainly” dairy and plant milks. FDA’s LACF line is 4.6, not 4.5 — use a process authority, not their marketing pH.

Retort is the default ambient path when acidity cannot carry the safety case: broths, plant milks, protein drinks, low-acid soups and meals, dairy RTD, baby-food pouches. Texture is cooked. That is the trade.

It is the wrong process when you are selling “never heated,” live cultures, or a refrigerated set — that is HPP. It is also the wrong process when the formula acidifies to pH ≤ 4.6 in time and can take a hot fill.

When retort is the wrong process

High-acid ambient SKUs that can take heat. Juice, tea, sports drink, many hot sauces. Heat plus acidity is the kill step. You do not need a filed LACF retort schedule. Use the hot-fill page.

HPP-sensitive / refrigerated SKUs. Cold-pressed juice, guacamole, “never cooked” salsa, live-culture drinks. HPP inactivates vegetative pathogens; spores survive; goods stay refrigerated. Thermal Kitchen’s FAQ: thermal uses heat; HPP uses pressure. Do not send ambient low-acid soup to an HPP toller, or a raw juice to a retort house.

Aseptic / UHT. Sterilize product and pack separately, then sterile-fill. Different capital than a retort. Big Brands lists aseptic as a separate process from retort.

Pack that cannot take the cook. Confirm retort-grade pouch, can, tray, or bottle. A hot-fill PET or HPP bottle is not a retort pack.

Low-acid scheduled process and process authority

Use FDA’s acidified / low-acid canned food framework (21 CFR 108 / 113), then make the plant prove they have a process authority letter for your formula. A “we do retort” page is not a filed schedule.

FDA’s LACF pages: commercial processors of shelf-stable acidified and low-acid canned foods in hermetically sealed containers sold in the US must register each establishment and file a scheduled process per product, style, container, and method (21 CFR 108). Acidified: Form FDA 2541e. Low-acid retorted: Form FDA 2541d (aw/formulation 2541f; aseptic 2541g).

Class Finished equilibrium pH aw What the plant must have Typical process
Acid food 4.6 or below without added acid usually > 0.85 Thermal process that kills vegetative pathogens/spoilage organisms in the pack Hot fill is common
Acidified food 4.6 or below after acid is added > 0.85 Registration + filed schedule (Form FDA 2541e) under 21 CFR 108/114; pH as a critical factor Hot fill if it acidifies in time
LACF greater than 4.6 > 0.85 Registration + filed schedule under 21 CFR 108/113 (retort: Form FDA 2541d) Retort or aseptic — not a hot-fill acid line
Refrigerated any any Cold chain; LACF/AF filing often does not apply if retailed refrigerated Cold fill, HPP, or hot fill then refrigerate

A thermal process authority (TPA) establishes the scheduled process. Nobody published a Form 2541d SID for a customer SKU.

USDA vs FDA is a different cut. Meat- or poultry-containing meals can fall under USDA FSIS. Treat that column as unpublished unless the plant states USDA-FSIS, USDA-inspected, or FDA-registered. Organic is not FSIS meat inspection.

Batch vs continuous, pouch vs can vs tray vs bottle

Generally true (not a plant quote): batch retort = load sealed containers, close the vessel, heat/hold/cool, unload. Continuous = containers move through heating, holding, and cooling zones. CoPack Connect describes both and names zero plants. Rotary / rotational retorts are agitated batch vessels, not continuous hydrostatic cookers.

This set: Berner rotary; Baxters static and rotational; Thermal Kitchen water-spray, gentle-motion. Big Brands “sterilization retorts” (type unpublished). Others name retort without vessel type. Continuous hydrostatic: unpublished for every plant here.
