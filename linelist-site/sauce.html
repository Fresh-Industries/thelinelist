How to read the MOQ column

Onofrio 100 cases. Motherlode (omitted) 400 gallons first trial. Chelten, Stir, Tulkoff, Sonoma, Paradigm, Best Brand: unpublished.

Convert before you rank plants. A 50-gallon Spice Guy run is not Supreme’s 1,000-gallon minimum.

If MOQ is unpublished, assume nothing. Do not treat unpublished national houses as small-MOQ plants.

Textbook — after the shortlist

What process your sauce actually needs

A sauce is not one process. Hot fill, cold fill, retort, and acidified kettle are different kill steps, different filings, and different plants.

Hot fill. Heated, filled hot, held, then cooled. Default ambient path for high-acid ketchup, hot sauce, many BBQ sauces and marinades. Not a retort shell.

Acidified kettle. Acid added so finished pH is 4.6 or below — acidified food under 21 CFR 108/114. Needs a process authority letter and Form FDA 2541e. The Spice Guy will not quote without one. 100-gallon kettles are equipment; FAQ floor is 50 gallons per flavor.

Cold fill. No hot-fill kill step. Parish hot-fills some sauces and cold-fills others. Supreme lists cold fill next to hot fill and retort.

Retort. Scheduled cook of the already-closed pack when pH > 4.6, aw > 0.85, hermetic, ambient — LACF (21 CFR 108/113). Supreme and Stir publish retort separately. Do not send LACF to a hot-fill acid line.

When this page is the wrong process

pH will not hold at or below 4.6 ambient. File a scheduled LACF process. Retort or aseptic — not a sauce hot-fill line.

“Never cooked,” live culture, or HPP-only claims. Heat destroys that story. Use the HPP page.

Dairy. Cream and cheese sauces are often low-acid. Yoshida (not in the table) refuses dairy because the Portland plant is Kosher Pareve. Ask for a dairy program and a process authority letter.

Meat or poultry. USDA FSIS vs FDA is not the pH table. Treat the USDA column as unpublished unless the plant states USDA-inspected or FDA-registered. Organic is not FSIS inspection.

Alcohol and tin cans. Plant-specific. Co-Packing Express: no potable alcohol, no tin cans.

Acid vs low-acid (operator version)

Use the FDA definitions, then make the plant prove a process authority letter for your formula. A “we do sauces” page is not a filed schedule.

FDA: register and file a scheduled process per product, style, container, and method (21 CFR 108). LACF: pH > 4.6, aw > 0.85 (excludes alcohol and tomatoes with pH < 4.7). Acidified: acid added to a low-acid food; finished pH ≤ 4.6, aw > 0.85. Do not assume salsa is “just acid.”

Class Finished equilibrium pH Water activity What the plant must have Typical sauce process
Acid food (naturally acid) 4.6 or below without added acid usually > 0.85 Thermal process that kills vegetative pathogens/spoilage organisms in the pack Hot fill is common (many fruit sauces, some tomato)
Acidified food 4.6 or below after acid is added > 0.85 Registration + filed schedule (Form FDA 2541e) under 21 CFR 108/114; pH as a critical factor Hot fill / acidified kettle if it acidifies in time
LACF greater than 4.6 > 0.85 Registration + filed schedule under 21 CFR 108/113 Retort or aseptic — not a standard hot-fill acid line
