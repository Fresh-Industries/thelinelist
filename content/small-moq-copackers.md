# Food Co-Packers With Small MOQs (What “Small” Actually Means)

“Small MOQ” is not a number. It is a process, a line, and a plant’s published floor — if they even publish one.

If you are an emerging CPG operator googling *food co-packer small MOQ*, the first page is a mix of plant sites pitching low minimums, RFQ platforms that hide the number until you log in, and at least one EU pouch house leaking into a US query. This page is the opposite of that: published US figures only, named plants only when they print an MOQ, and a blunt read of who will not take you.

**Last verified:** 21 Aug 2026 (CT). Search volume: **UNKNOWN**. We did not invent one.

## The SERP is selling hope. Plants sell line time.

On 21 Aug 2026, the *food co-packer small MOQ* query was bid by plant homepages, not by a public directory of minimums. [Minimus Products](https://minimusproducts.biz/) (Newbury Park, CA) still leads with a **$2,500 project minimum**. [IBR Packaging](https://ibrpackaging.com/industries/food/) (Carrollton, TX) still says it “regularly run[s] orders as small as 5,000 units.” [PartnerSlate](https://partnerslate.com/learningcenter/explaining-the-meaning-of-moq/) publishes category *estimates* and anonymous plant examples. [CoPack Connect](https://copackconnect.com/frequently-asked-questions/) matches you after you submit volume, format, and certs — it does not publish a browsable MOQ table. An EU plant (Doypacked.be) also appeared in this query in our teardown. It is not a US option.

If a plant did not print a number, this page writes **unpublished**. We do not guess a minimum. We do not list unpublished plants as “small-MOQ co-packers.”

If you cannot point to which row you are in, you are not ready to RFQ. A 25-gallon acidified sauce, a 5,000-unit sachet, a 7,500-liter hot-fill, and a 100,000-unit retort are four different businesses.

## Who will not take you

A plant that prints a six-figure unit floor is doing you a favor. Believe it.

**Retort / national beverage lines (published):**

- **Big Brands LLC** — “MOQ's Start at **100K Units per SKU**” on retort ([page](https://www.bigbrandsllc.com/retort-beverage-co-packing)). Same company: bottled lines **25,000 units** depending on fill size; 12 oz sleek PET cans **100,000/SKU**; kombucha **50,000/SKU**. Plants listed as California / Southern California; privacy-policy mailing address Las Vegas, NV. Do not argue sister-site “startup” copy against a published 100k retort floor.

**National sauce / condiment houses (MOQ unpublished on their own sites):**

Chelten House, Stir Foods, Tulkoff, and Supreme Cuisine rank for sauce co-man queries. On the public pages reviewed, **none published a numeric MOQ**. Unpublished ≠ small. A third-party Specialty Food Resource listing (not Tulkoff’s site) says the firm “does not work with start-up companies” and typical customers sell “approximately 16,000–18,000 gallons, or greater, per flavor” per year. Directory claim, not a plant-published floor. Do not staff a 25-gallon launch as if Chelten House is the path.

**HPP:** Universal Pure and Maryland Packaging did **not** publish a numeric brand MOQ on the pages reviewed. Throughput (kg/hour, vessel size) is not an MOQ. If your juice or dip needs HPP, you still have to ask. We will not invent a “startup HPP floor.”

**PartnerSlate high-end examples** (anonymous, still live): bars **100,000/SKU** with a 3–4 run annual commitment; granola **60,000–75,000 lbs**; dry seasonings **50,000 lbs**; pouched nut butter **25,000 units/SKU**.

**RFQ platforms:** CoPack Connect’s FAQ: you submit estimated volume; manufacturers “don’t have to scroll through listings.” PartnerSlate matches on volume bins behind a project listing. A login wall is not a published MOQ.

## How to show up ready (we are not brokering this draft)

Plants that print low minimums still waste a week on a founder who cannot answer the line questions. Bring this, in writing:

1. **Process, not vibe.** Hot-fill, retort, acidified kettle, cold-fill, HPP, dry blend, flexible pack, bakery. If you do not know, you are in a shared-use kitchen or a process authority consult, not a co-packer RFQ. Minimus will not hot-fill you. InnoMark will not carbonate you. IBR is a pouch/sachet/stick-pack house, not a 100k can retort.
2. **Formula that can be made on a line.** Finished commercial recipe, not a home batch. Ingredient list with suppliers if you have them, allergens, and whether you expect the plant to source or you will toll. InnoMark and Parish both sell R&D as a paid onboarding step; do not assume it is free.
3. **pH, aw, and scheduled process status.** Acidified and low-acid foods are not “sauce, but spicy.” If the plant files a scheduled process (Co-Packing Express says it supports this; confirm), they need a process authority letter, not a vibe. Bring pH. If you have never measured it, you are not production-ready.
4. **Packaging that exists.** Bottle/pouch drawing, fill weight, cap, film, label, case pack. IBR’s 4–6 week clock is mostly **film printing**. Custom components often have their own MOQ *above* the plant’s. A 5,000-unit fill is useless if the printed pouch MOQ is 25,000.
5. **A forecast, not a wish.** First-run quantity **and** 12-month replenishment. Parish Foods says they are not a one-time test run. IBR says they quote the quantity you order with no annual commitment — that is a different commercial shape. State which one you need. Include storage (cases, pallets, temperature) and sell-through so you are not asking a plant to warehouse a year of product they just made.
6. **Cash and certs.** Minimus and Co-Packing Express both publish dollar on-ramps ($2,500 project / $2,500–$3,000 test batch). InnoMark: 50% deposit with PO, 50% at delivery for new partners. Co-Packing Express: 75% non-refundable deposit. If organic, kosher, or GFSI (SQF) is a retailer requirement, match the badge in the table — Minimus prints CCOF + OK kosher; IBR prints SQF Level 2; InnoMark prints USDA Organic + kosher. Do not send an organic brief to a plant that did not publish organic.

A founder who finishes this page should be able to say one of: **25-gallon kettle sauce**, **5,000-unit pouch**, **~7,000–7,500 liter hot-fill**, **$2,500 pack-out**, or **not ready**. If your honest answer is “100k retort next quarter with no PO coverage,” you are not a small-MOQ customer. If your honest answer is “I have a jar and a farmers-market recipe,” you are still in a commercial kitchen.
