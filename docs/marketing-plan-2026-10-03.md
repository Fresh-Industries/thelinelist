# The Line List marketing plan

Prepared October 3, 2026. This is a proposed acquisition experiment, not a proven channel ranking. No campaigns, partnerships, posts, outreach, or spending were activated. Public copy follows `UX.md`: practical language for food and beverage founders, without age or generation labels.

## Positioning

**Food or drink idea? Make a plan for your first run.**

The promise is a clearer product plan and a better first manufacturer conversation. The distinctive proof is a persistent brief, explicit unknowns, public evidence behind manufacturer claims, and founder control over sharing and contact. Lead with the founder's job; show agent help and packaging exploration as supporting tools. Avoid promising that an idea is validated, a manufacturer can make it, a launch will succeed, or a low minimum is available without specific evidence.

Start with two focused audiences: beverage founders with a drink idea but incomplete formula/package decisions, and sauce or packaged-food founders moving beyond kitchen batches. Gen Z is an internal acquisition focus; need and product stage remain the public segmentation. Do not assume age predicts manufacturing readiness or willingness to pay. Keep conventional packaged food/beverage examples within the product-workspace scope; broad directory categories do not imply the workspace solves every manufacturing specialty.

This supports testing video distribution, not assuming those users are food founders or that impressions will convert. Its age band is not identical to Gen Z. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/)
- TikTok recommends vertical creative, an early hook, captions, a clear action, and a less polished style. Use real founder explanations and honest screen demonstrations, with one useful decision per video. Platform guidance is a starting hypothesis, not independent evidence of The Line List's results. [TikTok creative guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)
- Google recommends original, useful content with clear expertise and sources. Build on existing product guides and category pages rather than mass-generating keyword pages. [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Rutgers' Food Innovation Center supports early-stage food businesses with product development, business, and scale-up services. This is evidence that relevant founders gather around incubators and practical education. A workshop or resource referral is a channel to test; Rutgers is not a partner or endorsement. [Rutgers Food Innovation Center](https://foodinnovation.rutgers.edu/)
- TikTok offers keyword-based Search Ads, and its March 2026 Keyword Planner documentation describes volume and competition research. This makes intent-based testing possible if the account is eligible; search demand, cost, and founder quality have not been measured here. [Search Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-search-ads-campaign?lang=en), [Keyword Planner](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-keyword-planner-tool-in-tiktok-as-manager?lang=en)

Treat founder communities as places to answer specific questions with permission, not places for repeated link drops. Newsletter is an optional return path when configured, not the primary conversion. Keep manufacturer-side acquisition and founder acquisition separate.

## Ready-to-record hooks

Use fictional ideas clearly labelled “example product” and public profile facts with their source visible. A recorded demo must reflect the tested product version. Nik approves footage and factual claims before publishing.

| Hook | What to show | One action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Got a drink idea but no finished recipe? Start with these decisions.” | Enter an example drink idea; show recipe readiness and an open decision without inventing a formula | Start a product plan |
| “A manufacturer says it makes sauces. Does that include your hot sauce?” | One reviewed source, the specific product evidence, and an unknown package requirement | Read the hot-sauce guide |
| “Your first manufacturer email needs more than ‘I have an idea.’” | An example brief: product, recipe stage, package, first amount, storage; keep private fields excluded | Start a product plan |
| “Minimum order of 1,000 what? Units matter.” | A qualified published minimum and the right question; do not invent a price or promise a small run | Compare relevant manufacturer profiles |
| “Pretty packaging is a direction. It is not production approval.” | A supported package mockup and its saved state; explain what still needs manufacturer review | Explore the packaging decision in a plan |
| “What if none of the manufacturers fit?” | An honest no-results view, unresolved questions, and a reversible broader search | Explore the relevant directory |

Suggested 30-second first video: “Want to turn a drink idea into a brand? Your first manufacturing decision is simpler than a giant spec sheet. Here is an example: a sparkling mango drink. Start with what you know about the recipe, package, and first amount. Keep unknowns open. Then compare public evidence and ask the manufacturer what still needs confirmation. Start your product plan on The Line List.” This is a draft script; do not imply that the example has been produced or sourced successfully.

## Thirty-day experiment

1. **Days 1–7: verify the funnel.** Observe five consenting target founders on mobile: describe an idea, understand the next decision, distinguish an unknown from a supported capability, and review what an introduction would share. Do not send in a test. Confirm events reach the approved provider, establish current baselines, and fix material failures before paid traffic.
2. **Days 8–14: test two messages.** Use three videos each for “make a plan” and “prepare the first manufacturer conversation.” Keep audience, CTA destination, and production effort comparable. Preserve one clear CTA. Tag each creative with a generic campaign label, never the founder's idea or workspace token.
3. **Days 15–21: follow useful intent.** Make two follow-ups from actual questions. Prepare the workshop/checklist and an approved referral pilot. Publish or send only with Nik's authorization. Review source/capability claims immediately before publication.
4. **Days 22–30: evaluate quality.** Compare eligible sessions, successful plan creation, displayed research results, and drafts. Interview founders who progressed and who stopped. Choose the next experiment from observed problems; high views alone do not justify more spend.

A sample campaign URL after release is `/sourcing?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=first_run&utm_content=drink_recipe_01`. This is a proposed convention; confirm the approved provider captures attribution and preserves it through internal navigation. The discovery wizard preserves the five allowlisted UTM fields through progress and results; persistent attribution across the product-plan journey and return visits was not added. A guide-based video should link to its exact guide with the same generic convention.

## Funnel and decision rules

See [`analytics-events.md`](analytics-events.md) for implemented event semantics and gaps. Provider setup and dashboard access were not assumed.

| Stage | Measurement | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified visit | Eligible landing session by campaign; optional consented “what are you trying to make?” research | Reach alone does not identify a founder |
| Product-plan entry | `cta_product_plan_click`, with `source` | Homepage intent, not activation |
| Plan created | `product_plan_created`, with `entry` | Successful creation, not meaningful progress |
| Research result seen | `sourcing_matches_viewed`, with count/outcome | Split candidate and no-results experiences; repeat calls are not unique people |
| Draft prepared | `sourcing_drafts_prepared`, with count | A reversible draft, not contact or delivery |
| Founder usefulness / return | Consented research and deliberately approved future measurement | Not established by existing events |

Report eligible-session conversion rates with counts and date range. Keep manual and agent entry separate. Do not divide repeated event totals into a purported user funnel. Use no recipe text, contact details, private notes, workspace IDs, or packet links in analytics.

Initial operating rules are hypotheses, not industry benchmarks: pause traffic when creation fails, an unknown is presented as capability, private data enters tracking/sharing, or founders cannot identify the next decision. If 100 eligible visits produce fewer than five created plans, inspect message/landing continuity before spending more. If plans are created but five observed founders cannot reach useful evidence, improve activation before acquisition. With a small sample, use counts and interviews; do not declare a statistically proven winner. Choose one primary outcome per test and run a pre-specified, randomized experiment at an adequate sample once traffic supports it.

## Bounded resources

Begin with founder time: roughly 4–6 hours per week recording, answering real questions, and reviewing sessions. Use existing product assets and guides. Suggested optional research cap: $150 total for five $30 participant incentives. Suggested optional paid test cap: $300 total, at most $20/day for 15 days, on one channel. These are proposed ceilings in USD, not spend authorization or a cost forecast. Do not run several paid platforms at this scale. Confirm platform minimums, availability, and measurement before setting a campaign.

No revenue, pricing, lifetime value, manufacturer response rate, or paid conversion baseline was verified in this audit. An allowable customer acquisition cost cannot be responsibly calculated yet. Record cost per qualified created plan as a learning metric; do not call it customer acquisition cost. Keep the next month organic if tracking or activation cannot support a useful paid experiment.

## Changes made in this checkout

- Homepage now leads with the first-run planning outcome and a primary **Start my product plan** action to the canonical `/sourcing` workflow. **Browse by product** moves prepared visitors to the category selector.
- The four-step explanation follows idea → plan → public manufacturer evidence → founder-reviewed introduction.
- Category cards retain direct directory entry. Unsupported popularity language and an unmeasured “60-second” quiz promise were removed.
- Home metadata uses the practical promise; approved static social previews are preserved. Existing canonical URLs and indexability rules stay intact.
- Privacy-safe plan-entry, creation, research-result, and draft milestones have named event definitions. No provider was enabled and no live campaign outcomes are claimed.

Release validation and remaining workflow limitations are recorded by the accompanying audit. Local implementation is not hosted verification. This plan is ready for Nik's editorial review; campaigns, community posts, outreach, and spending remain proposals.
